## The short version

1. **TensorRT-LLM BF16 is slower because of attention, not matrix multiplies.**
   - At matched batch sizes its decode step is longer by:
     - +0.16 ms at batch 1;
     - +1.3 ms at 16;
     - +1.3 to +3.1 ms at chat's 21–27;
     - +7.0 ms at 64.
   - Attention is 76–95% of that gap. TensorRT-LLM's decode-attention kernel (XQA,
     `kernel_mha`) takes 1.14–1.19x the time of vLLM's FlashAttention split-KV kernel.
   - From 16 to 64 in flight each extra sequence costs 0.435 ms vs 0.316 ms (1.38x),
     matching the client-side fit (0.455 vs 0.336).
2. **The generic SM80 GEMM kernel is not the cause.** vLLM runs the same
   `cutlass_80_wmma_tensorop_bf16_s161616gemm` kernels: both engines get them from cuBLAS
   through PyTorch.
   - GEMM time per step is within 2–3% at every batch.
   - At batch 1 it is identical: 20.85 ms, 84% of the L40S's memory bandwidth.
3. **The FP8 cliff is the CUDA-graph batch sizes, and padding removes it.**
   - On the same seeded arrival traces, stock FP8 collapsed in 3 of 6 runs. It collapsed
     on the same 3 in both of its runs (08204560 and ad5b1408), with ITL p50 49–51 ms or
     TPOT p95 48–54 ms and the batch peaking at 72–74.
   - Padded FP8 ran all 6 at ITL p50 17–18 ms and TPOT p95 23–25 ms.
   - Padded goodput is at least 3.375 req/s (not bracketed), at $0.1237 per 1M blended
     tokens. That is at most 0.67x vLLM BF16's cost.
   - It is a comparison point, not a ranked row. Its gate was GSM8K plus divergence only,
     and stock FP8's tool_calling_strict failure applies to the same engine.
4. **Eager decode steps are what make the cliff.** In the BF16 trace, decode-only steps at
   batches without a graph (35, 42, 49, 56) left the GPU idle 12–64 ms per step. With a
   graph the idle was about 0.1 ms. For FP8, whose GPU work per step is about 17–20 ms,
   that launch time decides the step.
5. **A separate FP8 problem: at batch 4 or less, with an FP8 KV cache, attention falls
   back to the slow MMHA kernel.**
   - Batch 4 took 79.5 ms per step, 67.4 ms of it in MMHA. Batch 5 took 14.0 ms with XQA.
   - It is not caused by padding: stock FP8's own smoke ran at 38–79 ms ITL at batches 1–3.
   - It affects light load only; every step at the loads that set goodput used XQA.

## Not established

- **Why XQA is slower than FlashAttention on sm_89 for BF16 KV.** The torch trace times
  kernels but cannot see bandwidth or occupancy (Nsight Compute could).
- **Whether another TensorRT-LLM attention backend closes the gap.** Not run.
- **Exact context lengths in the 1k-1k windows.** These are closed loops whose requests
  start together, so contexts vary with timing. The chat windows, open loop at near-equal
  load, are the fairest pairing and agree on direction.
- **The FP8 cliff state itself, in a trace.** Both FP8 windows at 3.375 ran after the
  measured runs, at batches 15–30, where both configs have graphs. Their steps match to
  within 0.1 ms.
- **Where padded FP8's knee is,** and whether an explicit batch list without padding does
  as well.
- **The exact rule that picks MMHA over XQA in rc2's dispatch path.** The
  `mayHavePerfGain` heuristic (KV heads x batch x multi-block count vs 142 SMs) is the
  likely one, but it is not confirmed.
